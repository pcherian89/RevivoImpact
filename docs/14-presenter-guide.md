# 14 · Presenter guide: showing Revivo Impact

This guide is for anyone presenting the Revivo Impact prototype. You don't need any technical knowledge.

Revivo Impact is a **prototype on fictional data**. Every person, organisation, amount and record is invented. No real
money moves, nobody really signs in, and nothing is sent anywhere. Say this at the start; the banner on every screen
says it too.

## 1. Opening it

1. Open the file **`revivo-impact-final.html`** (double-click it). It opens in your web browser: Chrome, Edge, Safari
   or Firefox. It needs no internet connection, no installation and no account.
2. You land on the **start page**. It explains both sides in one screen:
   - **"I fund sport"**: foundations, companies, brands, government bodies, federations, individual sponsors.
   - **"I'm looking for funding"**: athletes, parents and guardians, coaches, clubs and programmes.
3. Before a presentation, press **Reset** (see §8) so the demonstration starts clean.

**Screen size.** It works on a phone, a tablet and a laptop. For a room, use a laptop at full screen. On a phone the
guide sits at the bottom of the screen and shrinks to one line while you look at a screen.

**Dark or Daylight.** The sun button in the top bar switches between the dark look and the brighter Daylight look.
Use Daylight on a projector in a bright room. Printing always uses Daylight.

## 2. The top bar (always there)

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Prototype** (top left) | Back to the start page. |
| **Guided journeys** | Opens the guide beside the page (on phones: inside the "Demo" menu). |
| **Explore by role** | Lists every person in the story. Pick one to see the product through their eyes (on phones: inside the "Demo" menu). |
| **Demo date** | Moves the story to one of three dates: Mon 12 Apr, Thu 6 May or Wed 30 Jun 2027. It also holds **Reset the demonstration**, which asks once before clearing anything. |
| Sun button | Dark or Daylight. |

**Whose screen is this?** Under the menu of every screen, one line says who you are looking at: their name, their
role and their organisation, for example "Viewing as Ingrid Solberg · Funding approver · Tallwater Sports Foundation"
or "Viewing as Jonas Becker · Swimming coach · Saltmere Sports Academy · acting for Tiago (16)". **Change person**
on the same line switches to someone else.

## 3. The guided journey (the main demonstration)

Press **"Start the guided journey"** on the start page. The guide opens beside the page (on a laptop) or at the
bottom (on a phone or tablet). Its tabs say what each journey is about: **A · Scholarship**, **B · Sponsors**,
**C · Programmes**, **D · Existing funding**, **E · Young athletes**.

**Journey A: Find and secure a sports scholarship.** 14 steps, about 10 minutes. Follow Amira, 19, a judo athlete who
has never heard of Tallwater Sports Foundation:

| Step | What the audience sees |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tallwater publishes its scholarship. Daniel drafted it; Ingrid, a different person, publishes it. |
| 2 | Amira joins on her own, with no invitation. |
| 3 | She builds one sporting profile and asks her federation to confirm her national bronze. |
| 4 | She discovers five opportunities from five organisations and sees exactly why she qualifies. |
| 5–6 | Simulated AI fills the application from her profile, flags a missing invoice, and she submits. |
| 7 | Daniel checks her age against her ID, scores her application and shortlists her for the $4,500 award. |
| 8 | Ingrid decides every offer. A meter shows the budget after each decision. Nobody is turned down while still being reviewed. |
| 9 | Amira reads what Tallwater will see, and accepts. $4,500 agreed. |
| 10 | Priya in finance (not the person who approved it) records the first payment, $2,250 (simulated). Amira confirms it arrived. |
| 11 | Amira adds her spending and her 5th place, with the results sheet. |
| 12 | Priya checks three receipts and asks for the missing one. Daniel decides: continue with conditions. |
| 13 | Meanwhile, a company finds an athlete it has never met (two-way discovery). |
| 14 | A foundation watches a whole programme, site by site. |

**How each step works.** Each step says whose screen you will see ("You see the screen of Priya Nair · Finance
officer · Tallwater Sports Foundation"), who takes part when more than one person acts, and what happens.
- **Show me**: the prototype does the step for you, through the same rules as a click.
- **I'll do it**: the screen opens and you click through it yourself. The step is marked done as soon as what it
  describes has happened, however you did it. A done step says so, and there is nothing to repeat.
- **Move to … and continue**: appears when a step is on a later date. It moves the story forward and fills in the
  earlier steps for you.
- Each finished step lists **What changed**: the records it created, and who did what.

**The ending.** After the last step, **What you just saw** sums up the value in a few lines. Then choose:
**Next: journey B** (sets the story to where journey B begins), **Start again** (clears everything and starts journey A
again) or **Back to the start page**. Every journey can also be started on its own from its tab: "Move to … and
continue" fills in anything it depends on.

## 4. The supporting journeys (B to E)

Use the A–E tabs at the top of the guide.

| Journey | Steps, time | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **B** Two-way discovery, direct support and sponsorship | 7 steps, about 5 minutes | Nadia (23, wheelchair racing) publishes a funding request. She asks an individual sponsor directly; a company finds her. Two funders fund part each, so nothing is funded twice. A brand proposes a sponsorship agreement to Jonah (22, sprinter), kept apart from development money. |
| **C** Sports organisations: funding and programme monitoring | 8 steps, about 6 minutes | A club funded by a sponsor it did not know. A programme records sessions on a phone. A site falls behind; a person acts and a second person approves the recovery plan. An honest quarterly report. |
| **D** Bring existing funding onto Revivo Impact | 4 steps, about 3 minutes | Funding that started before Revivo Impact joins the same records, without a new application. |
| **E** Coach, guardian and safeguarding | 6 steps, about 5 minutes | A guardian decides everything for an under-18. Funders see only an anonymised card. A coach prepares but can never accept or change consent. Withdrawing consent works at once. |

## 5. Switching roles (hands-on)

Press **Explore by role**. Every person in the story is listed by group (seeking funding, delivering programmes,
providing funding, platform), with two or three things to try. Pick one to see their screens. Each person has at most
five menu items, and their first screen starts with **Needs your action** (at most three things).

Good people to show:
- **Amira** (athlete): Home, Discover, My Funding.
- **Rosa** (guardian of Tiago, 16, and Inês, 13): Consent & safety.
- **Ingrid** (Funding approver, Tallwater): Funding overview, Selection, the funder report.
- **Priya** (Tallwater finance): payments coming up, record a payment.
- **Kenji** (Oriel, a company): Discover with a plain-words brief.
- **Sam** (programme coach): recording a session on a phone.
- **Alex** (platform administrator): safeguarding reviews and the audit of who did what.

The **Demo date** changes what each person sees. 6 May is the busiest date for decisions; 30 June shows spending,
reports and reviews.

## 6. Two-way discovery

- **Athletes and organisations find funders:** Discover → "Funding opportunities" and "Sponsors & funders". Each result
  says why it appears ("Why: supports para sport (includes wheelchair racing) · any region · accepts direct requests up
  to $2,500").
- **Funders find athletes, requests and teams:** Discover for a funder. Type a plain-words brief, for example "para
  athletes needing equipment"; it becomes filters you can edit. Results are ordered by how many filters each meets,
  and each says what it meets and what it does not. Never by popularity, followers or any "talent" score.
- Revivo Impact shows **participating** organisations only. It never claims to list every sponsor.
- Under-18s are never searchable. Funders see only an anonymised card that a guardian chose to publish.

## 7. The three Simulated AI experiences

Each is labelled **"Simulated AI"**. Each is built from fixed rules over the records the person may see, and never
decides anything.

1. **Matching explanations** (Discover): why each opportunity or sponsor fits, and what is still missing.
2. **Application help** ("Help me organise this"): fills the form from the profile, flags missing documents, drafts
   a statement from the records. It never replaces words the person typed; it offers a suggestion instead.
3. **Ask Revivo** (funder Overview): answers questions such as "Which payments are coming up?" from the funder's own
   records, with the source of every figure. It refuses to rank people, judge potential, decide funding, identify
   young people, or answer about another funder.

## 8. Funding, payments and reporting

- Every funding arrangement has **one funding record** with a **Funding Line**: Agreed → Paid → Received → Spent →
  Checked → Progress → Reviewed.
- **Every figure opens its sources** (underlined numbers and "See where this comes from").
- Money words stay plain: **Agreed**, **Paid**, **Paid, not yet spent**, **Spent, checked by the funder**, **Still to
  come**. A promise is never shown as paid; reported spending is never shown as checked.
- **Recording a payment** (simulated): finance enters the amount, the date paid and an optional reference. Payments
  cannot run ahead of the schedule or its conditions.
- **Commercial sponsorship** is always counted apart from development and programme money.
- **The funder report** (Ingrid → Funding → Reports, or the funder report screen) adds up by fund and by sport, can be
  downloaded as a CSV file, and carries the honesty line: "Delivery and spending records show what was delivered and
  spent; they do not on their own prove social impact."
- **The claims check** on reports flags amounts, counts and words such as "transformed" that the records don't
  support. It can't prove every sentence is true, and it says so.

## 9. Resetting

Two ways, both clear everything done in the demonstration and return to the start:
- **Demo date → Reset the demonstration** (any time).
- **Start again** at the end of a journey.

The prototype remembers your clicks in this browser until you reset. Nothing leaves the computer.

## 10. What is simulated (say this plainly)

| Simulated | What a real system would need |
|---|---|
| Sign-in and email codes | Real accounts and secure sign-in |
| Identity, organisation and safeguarding checks | Real checks by qualified people and services |
| Payments ("recorded", never moved) | A bank or payment provider; finance controls |
| Receipts and documents (fictional previews) | Real document upload and storage |
| The three AI experiences (fixed rules, labelled "Simulated AI") | A real AI service with the same limits and refusals |
| Notifications | Email or text messages |
| Storage (in this browser only) | A secure database |

## 11. Limitations to mention if asked

- Fictional data only; US dollars only; English only.
- Of the 143 scholarship applications, 11 open in full; the others show their scores and status only.
- On a fresh 30 June there is no waiting receipt to check until the guide (or a person) adds spending.
- Some screens use slightly different content widths on very wide screens.
- This is a prototype: it has not been tested with real users. A plan for short human sessions is in the QA report
  (`15-p5-qa-report.md` §9).

## 12. If something goes wrong

- **The screen looks odd or a step won't run:** Reset (§9) and start the journey again.
- **The guide covers something on a phone:** press the small arrow to shrink it to one line, or close it with ✕.
- **You are on the wrong person's screen:** press "Viewing as …" and choose the right person.
