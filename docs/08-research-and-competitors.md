# Revivo Impact — research and competitors

From P0 Panel A (market, competitors, commercial model; web research on 24 Nov 2026), with the v1 competitor notes
and the owner's earlier strategy evidence (archive). Strategy conclusions drawn from this research are in `01`.

**Evidence labels.** **[V]** verified in a vendor's own page or help centre, or an official or government source, as
shown in search excerpts (cited). **[V-3p]** third-party listing (G2, Capterra, Dealroom and similar); indicative only.
**[J]** judgement. **Limitation:** direct page fetches to foundant.com, upshot.org.uk and submittable.com were blocked
in the research session, so those vendors are verified through search excerpts of their own pages, not full reads.
Nothing here says a competitor "lacks" a feature; where something was not found, it says "not seen in sources
reviewed". Re-verify before quoting any figure externally.

## 1. Bottom line

1. The founder's brief joins two businesses: Routes A and D (a funder runs scholarships, grants or programmes, then
   tracks money and progress) are coherent institutional software; Routes B and C (athletes post needs; sponsors
   browse athletes) are a two-sided marketplace with different buyers, budgets, law and economics. Lead with A and D;
   keep B and C secondary. [J]
2. Generic scholarship and grant software is a crowded, inexpensive category (SmarterSelect from about $1–2k a year;
   Good Grants about €3.2–6.4k; Foundant about $4–14k). "Publish → apply → review → award → report" is parity. Revivo
   must win on sport-specific value: verified athlete fields, guardian-proxied minors, nominations, money states and
   sporting progress after the award. [V-3p, J]
3. Strongest first use case: a foundation or CSR team runs an athlete scholarship round from a shareable link and
   follows each award. Programme monitoring is module two. [J]

**Notes [J].** Grant tools and athlete marketplaces barely overlap: different budgets (programme or CSR versus
marketing) and outputs (evidence of use versus proof of exposure). Upshot and Views own *delivery* data; generic grant
tools own *intake and award*. The credible gap is the join **for individual athletes**: award → released → spent →
verified → sporting progress → renewal. Matching, AI summaries, reviewer portals, dashboards and payments are table
stakes.

### 2.1 Earlier competitor notes (v1, Nov 2026; programme monitoring)

- **Upshot** (UK, from the Football Foundation; monitoring, attendance, participants, outcomes, funder reporting;
  turnover-based pricing) and **Views by Substance** (mobile attendance, participants, surveys, outcomes, case
  studies; Liverpool FC Foundation, Greenhouse Sports) are direct competitors for programme monitoring.
- Also relevant: **JustGo** (membership and registration, 100+ national governing bodies), **Rosterfy** (volunteers
  and events), **SponsorCX** (sponsorship), **Salesforce Nonprofit**, **Makerble**.
- Conclusion kept: offline registers, mobile attendance, dashboards, surveys, photo evidence and AI-drafted reports are
  standard; match them, do not sell them. v1's distinctive elements, now inside Revivo Impact: the
  Commitment-to-Evidence Chain linking spending to delivery; early warnings that lead to action (a missed session
  starts a reason → recovery plan → approval workflow); per-purpose consent; designed for places incumbents serve less
  (regional pricing, simple phone use).
- Owner's earlier problem evidence (archive `v1-04`): Sport England's evaluation framework; UK government evaluation
  of the Sport Survival Package (complaints about monitoring forms and producing financial information); a 2026
  academic study on sport-for-development organisations lacking evaluation funding and expertise; India CSR
  disclosures and impact assessments. The root problem is fragmentation (paper registers, messaging-app updates,
  spreadsheets, accounting software, photo folders, slide-deck reports), so reports are late; the value is early
  visibility. This validates the problem, not willingness to pay.

**Patterns modelled in the fictional world [J]:** nomination routes alongside open calls; age bands; need plus
performance; small fixed awards ($/£500–10,000); caps and "no double funding" declarations; out-of-pocket evidence;
12-month terms with a mid-term review; award agreements with reporting conditions; split support (a direct allowance
plus organisation-managed costs). Hence the Emerging Athletes Scholarship uses tiers ($3,500 / $4,500 / $5,000), a
nomination route and an "Other funding" declaration (`05` §4).

## 4. Legal and finance notes (design considerations, never compliance claims)

- **US private foundations:** grants to individuals need IRS advance approval and an objective, non-discriminatory
  selection process (s.4945(g)) [V].
- **India CSR:** sport training is a CSR activity under Schedule VII item (vii) [V]; CSR normally flows through
  registered implementing agencies (check before any India pilot) [J, needs legal check]; administrative overheads are
  capped (check) [J].
- **Minors:** endorsement contracts with minors are generally voidable; some US states require parental consent or
  court approval; rules differ by state [V law-firm and statute sources]. Rule adopted: commercial sponsorship of
  under-18s only through a guardian (or an organisation), never shown in public discovery [J].
- **Money, tax and jurisdiction:** holding or moving funds makes a platform a payments or charity intermediary (identity
  checks, licensing, tax receipts; compare Submittable's partner-run payouts and the Australian Sports Foundation's
  tax status). **Revivo records commitments and disbursements; it never moves money** [J].
- **Data protection:** v1 designed children's data for UK GDPR and India's DPDP Act (verifiable parental consent) as a
  design consideration only, never a compliance claim. The same applies here.

## 6. Verified versus judgement

Verified [V]: competitor features and prices as cited; real programme amounts and criteria; s.4945(g); India CSR
Schedule VII (vii); minors' contract rules in cited sources. Judgement [J]: the two-business analysis, the strongest
use case, the differentiation hypotheses, pricing hypotheses, procurement routes, risks and the go/no-go thresholds.
Nothing in this file is evidence of demand or willingness to pay; that is what the interviews in `01` §10 test.
